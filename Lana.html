Smallville Safety Plan – Lana Lang Edition

A calm, structured plan inspired by Lana Lang’s strength, created in full sentences for real-life safety and support.

“Even in Smallville, storms pass. This plan helps you stay grounded until the sky clears.”

This safety plan is written as if Lana Lang is standing beside you and speaking calmly. It uses complete sentences and clear steps so that you can follow it even when you feel overwhelmed.

1. My current situation and early warning signs

I begin by noticing what is happening around me and inside me. I pay attention to my thoughts, my emotions, and my body. I remind myself that noticing these signs early is a strength, not a weakness.

Early warning signs I want to watch for

  • I notice that my thoughts become very fast, very negative, or very hopeless, and I feel as if everything is too much.
  • I feel tightness in my chest or stomach, and I notice that my breathing becomes shallow or rushed.
  • I start to withdraw from people who care about me, and I ignore messages or calls even when I want support.
  • I feel an urge to act impulsively, to run away, or to hurt myself, and I recognize that this is a sign that I need help and calm, not judgment.

When I notice these signs, I say to myself in a clear sentence: “I am noticing warning signs, and I choose to follow my safety plan now.”

2. My immediate calming steps (Smallville-style grounding)

I use simple, concrete actions to help my mind and body slow down. I choose at least one step and I describe it to myself in full sentences so that I stay focused.

Calming actions I can take right away

  • I take ten slow breaths, and with each breath I imagine standing in a quiet Smallville field, feeling the wind move gently through the corn.
  • I look around the room and name five things I can see, four things I can touch, three things I can hear, two things I can smell, and one thing I can taste, and I say each item out loud or in my mind.
  • I drink a glass of water slowly, and I remind myself that I am caring for my body in this moment.
  • I sit or lie down in a safe place, and I tell myself: “Right now I am choosing safety, and I will stay here until I feel more stable.”

I do not need to fix everything at once. I only need to take the next small step toward calm.

3. My safe people and safe places

I remind myself that I do not have to face difficult moments alone. I think of the people and places that feel most like the Talon café or the Kent farm—steady, warm, and safe.

People I can contact for support

  • I can call or message a trusted friend or family member and say in a full sentence: “I am having a hard time right now, and I would really appreciate it if you could stay on the line or chat with me for a while.”
  • I can reach out to a professional helper, such as a therapist, counselor, or support worker, and I can say: “I am following my safety plan, and I need some guidance and reassurance.”
  • If I feel that I might be in immediate danger, I can contact emergency services and clearly say: “I am not feeling safe right now, and I need urgent help.”

Places that feel safer to me

  • I can move to a room or area in my home where I feel most calm, and I stay there with my phone nearby.
  • If it is safe to do so, I can go outside to a familiar place, such as a park, a quiet street, or a favorite café, and I stay aware of my surroundings.
  • I avoid places where I feel trapped, judged, or at risk, and I remind myself that choosing a safer location is an act of courage.

4. My safety rules for myself

I create clear rules in full sentences that protect me when my emotions feel intense. These rules are promises I make to myself, and I treat them as seriously as Lana would treat a promise to someone she loves.

  • I will not act on any urge to harm myself or others. Instead, I will tell someone I trust about the urge and ask for help staying safe.
  • I will remove or move away from any objects that I could use to hurt myself, and I will place them out of reach or in another room.
  • I will not use alcohol or drugs to cope with intense feelings, because they can make my situation more dangerous and more confusing.
  • I will keep my phone charged and nearby so that I can contact support quickly if I need it.

If I break one of these rules or come close to breaking one, I will not shame myself. Instead, I will say: “I still deserve safety and care, and I am returning to my plan right now.”

5. My reasons to stay and my Smallville-style hope

I remind myself why my life matters, even when my mind tries to convince me otherwise. I think of the way Lana keeps going despite loss and fear, and I connect that strength to my own story.

Reasons I choose to stay alive and stay safe

  • I have people, pets, or future relationships that I care about, and I want to give myself the chance to experience more moments with them.
  • There are things I still want to see, learn, and create, and I know that my story is not finished yet.
  • I have survived difficult days before, and that proves that I have strength, even when I do not feel it.
  • I deserve the possibility of feeling better, and I will not take that possibility away from myself.

I can write or say a sentence of hope, such as: “This moment is painful, but it is not the end of my story.”

Smallville-style self-talk

I can use these example sentences as if Lana is speaking to me, or as if I am speaking to myself with the same kindness she would offer.

  • “I am allowed to feel what I feel, and I am still worthy of safety and care.”
  • “I do not have to be perfect to deserve help. I only have to be honest about how I feel.”
  • “I am not alone in this. There are people and resources that can stand with me.”
  • “My pain is real, but it is not the only truth about me.”

I can choose one sentence that feels most comforting and repeat it slowly until my body begins to relax even a little.
