🍔🌭Hamburger & Hot Dog Style Lab

Use classic cookout favorites to teach planning, sequencing, and even a bit of math. Each card is a mini-lesson plus a simple recipe idea.

Build-a-Burger Story

Hands-on Ages 8–14

Ask youth to design a “dream burger” and turn the steps into a story: who is eating it, where, and why.

  • Step 1: Choose bun, patty, and toppings.
  • Step 2: Write each step as a sentence.
  • Step 3: Add a beginning, problem, and fun ending.

Hot Dog Fraction Line

Math Focus Ages 9–15

Use sliced hot dogs (or paper cutouts) to show fractions and division.

  • Idea: Cut a hot dog into equal pieces and label each piece as a fraction.
  • Challenge: “If we share 3 hot dogs among 4 friends, how much does each get?”

Menu Math

Real-world Math Group Work

Create a simple burger & hot dog menu with prices. Let youth “order” and calculate totals, change, and discounts.

  • Skill: Addition, subtraction, and percent discounts.
  • Extension: Design a combo meal and compare savings.

🥗🌮Taco Salad Learning Station

Taco salad is perfect for teaching choice, categories, and step-by-step thinking. Use it as a metaphor for building ideas.

Taco Salad Recipe Map

Creative Thinking Ages 8–16

Draw a “recipe map” where each ingredient is a branch. Youth choose ingredients and then write directions in order.

  • Base: Lettuce, chips, or rice.
  • Protein: Beans, chicken, beef, tofu.
  • Extras: Cheese, salsa, corn, avocado.

Math Mix-ins

Ratios & Fractions

Turn the salad into a ratio lesson: “2 scoops of beans for every 3 scoops of lettuce.”

  • Prompt: “If we double the recipe, what happens to each ingredient?”
  • Visual: Use colored counters to represent each ingredient.

🧠📚Teaching How to Teach

This section is for mentors and youth leaders. It outlines a simple structure for planning any lesson: food, math, or storytelling.

4-Step Teaching Framework

  • 1. Hook: Start with something familiar (a burger, a game, a place they love).
  • 2. Explore: Let youth talk, guess, and try before you explain.
  • 3. Explain: Give clear, short instructions or definitions.
  • 4. Reflect: Ask, “What did we learn?” and “Where could we use this?”

Teaching Checklist

  • Goal: What should they be able to do at the end?
  • Materials: What do you need ready?
  • Time: How long will each part take?
  • Support: Who can help students who get stuck?

🎬📝Storyboard Structure for Youth

A storyboard is a visual plan for a story, video, or project. Here’s a simple length and structure you can teach.

Panel 1 – Setting

Who is in the scene? Where are they? What time of day is it? (Example: friends at a summer cookout.)

Panel 2 – Problem

Something goes wrong or needs to be solved. (Example: they don’t have enough hot dogs for everyone.)

Panel 3 – Plan

Characters come up with a plan. (Example: they create taco salads from extra ingredients.)

Panel 4 – Action

Show the plan in motion—draw or describe the steps.

Panel 5 – Solution

How is the problem solved? What did they learn?

Panel 6 – Reflection

One sentence about the message of the story: sharing, teamwork, or creative problem-solving.

➗📏Math for Youth – Fun Approaches

Connect math to everyday things—food, games, and places. Here are quick lesson ideas you can plug into your teaching.

Cookout Fractions

Use paper plates and cut-out burgers/hot dogs to show halves, thirds, and quarters.

  • Activity: “Shade 1/2 of the toppings” or “Share 3 burgers among 5 friends.”

Taco Salad Graphs

Let youth vote on favorite ingredients and turn the results into a bar graph or pie chart.

  • Skill: Reading and creating graphs.
  • Extension: Compare two groups’ favorites.

Place-to-See Coordinates

Draw a simple map of a city or park and label spots with coordinates.

  • Activity: “Walk” from one place to another using steps on the grid.

🤝💡Helping Hands – Group Names & Roles

Create small groups with positive names and clear roles so youth feel ownership of the learning space.

Group Name Ideas

  • Math Chefs – mix numbers like ingredients.
  • Story Grillers – cook up new stories.
  • Taco Thinkers – layer ideas like taco toppings.
  • Helping Hands Crew – focused on kindness and support.

Roles in Each Group

  • Leader: Keeps the group on task.
  • Recorder: Writes or draws the group’s ideas.
  • Speaker: Shares the group’s work with others.
  • Helper: Checks that everyone understands.
