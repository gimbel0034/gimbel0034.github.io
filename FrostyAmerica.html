Frosty Snowflakes, Snowmen & Olaf

Winter Magic

Snowflakes & Snowmen

Frosty snowflakes drift through the air, each one unique and delicate. When they gather on the ground, they invite children and families to build snowmen together.

A snowman is more than snow; it is a moment of joy, a shared laugh, and a memory that melts slowly into spring.

  • Roll three snowballs: small, medium, and large.
  • Stack them carefully to form the snowman’s body.
  • Add a carrot nose, buttons, and a warm scarf.

Snowball Recipes – Sweet Winter Treats

Recipe

Classic Coconut Snowballs

  • Mix powdered sugar, butter, and vanilla until smooth.
  • Shape into small balls and chill.
  • Roll in shredded coconut to create a “snowy” look.

These treats look like tiny snowballs and bring winter cheer to any gathering.

Healthy Twist

Fruit & Yogurt Snowballs

  • Combine Greek yogurt with finely chopped fruit.
  • Freeze in small scoops on a tray.
  • Dust lightly with coconut or crushed cereal.

A lighter option that still feels festive and fun, perfect for school or family celebrations.

Different Animals – Bears & Wolves

Wildlife

Bears

Bears are strong, curious animals that live in forests and mountains. They search for berries, fish, and shelter, moving carefully through their habitat.

  • Respect their space and observe from a safe distance.
  • Store food properly when camping.
  • Learn local safety guidelines for wildlife.
Wildlife

Wolves

Wolves travel in packs, communicating with howls and body language. They work together to survive, showing the power of teamwork and family.

  • Wolves help keep ecosystems balanced.
  • They rely on cooperation and trust within the pack.
  • They remind us that community matters in life circumstances.

Safety Plan for Everyday Life Circumstances

A simple safety plan helps students, families, and communities feel prepared. It can be used at school, at home, and while exploring lakes, rivers, and mountains.

Observe Your Surroundings: Notice exits, safe areas, and trusted adults wherever you are.
Share Your Plan: Tell a family member or teacher where you are going and when you will return.
Carry Essentials: Keep important phone numbers, a small flashlight, and any needed medication with you.
Stay Connected: Use the buddy system when walking, hiking, or attending events.
Reflect & Improve: After drills or real situations, talk about what worked and what could be better.
